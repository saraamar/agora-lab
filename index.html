The animating question

When AI and automation enter an operation, a factory, a cooperative, a trading corridor ... who gains decision-making power and who loses it?

This question runs through every project in AGORA Lab. The methods change â€” operations research, field experiments, multi-criteria decision analysis, management science. The question does not.

Operations & management first. AGORA is grounded in industrial and systems engineering, operations management, and management science. AI is a tool we study â€” not the discipline we practice.

Applied over abstract. Every AGORA project has a field component, an industry partner, or a real decision-maker as a stakeholder. Methods serve problems, not the other way around.

Advanced methods, broader contexts. AGORA examines how modern operations management and manufacturing practices â€” Industry 5.0, AI-assisted decision-making, optimization, lean systems â€” perform across a wide range of organizational scales and settings, from advanced manufacturers to SMEs worldwide. The comparison between what works in well-resourced environments and what transfers to constrained ones is where the most generalizable and impactful knowledge is produced.

Industry 5.0 as the frame. Human-centricity and resilience over automation-first efficiency. A system that optimizes throughput while excluding workers is not a well-designed system.

MANGO
Manufacturing & Operations
Game · AGORA Lab
2026 KEEN Engineering Unleashed Fellow · $10,000 Grant · Kern Family Foundation
⚙

Production Planning

Teams manage a live production schedule, balancing capacity, lead times, and customer demand across multiple product lines and time horizons.

📈

MRP & Inventory Control

Material Requirements Planning drives real order decisions. Students feel the difference between just-in-time and safety-stock strategies under demand uncertainty.

⚒

Supplier Selection

Multi-criteria supplier decisions under cost, quality, and lead time trade-offs — with dynamic conditions that change mid-game to reflect real sourcing complexity.

⚡

Disruption Management

Unexpected events — supplier failures, demand spikes, logistics delays — force teams to adapt in real time, building resilience thinking beyond static models.

🏆

Competitive Team Play

Teams compete against each other on a shared market, creating genuine strategic tension. Decisions are interdependent — what one team does affects every other.

📈

Entrepreneurial Mindset

Developed under the KEEN Engineering Unleashed framework, integrating opportunity recognition, value creation, and systems thinking into the engineering education experience.

How a MANGO session works

01

Brief & team formation

Students receive their company roles, initial inventory, budget, and market conditions. Teams self-organize across production, procurement, and logistics functions.

02

Round play

Each round represents a planning period. Teams simultaneously make supplier, production, and inventory decisions using the browser-based interface — then a market simulation resolves outcomes.

03

Disruption events

Mid-game events — a supplier quality failure, an unexpected order surge, a logistics disruption — force real-time adaptation and expose the fragility of over-optimized systems.

04

Debrief & analysis

Post-game data review connects team decisions to outcomes. Students see, quantitatively, how their planning choices propagated through the system — and what they would do differently.

05

Classroom integration

MANGO is designed to complement ISE and operations management curricula. Facilitation guides, rubrics, and learning objectives are provided for instructors.
